# Stakeholder Briefing

## Executive Message

Victorian public transport patronage has recovered substantially from the COVID disruption period, but recovery is uneven across modes. In 2025, total patronage reached 490.2 million trips, or 81.3% of the 2019 baseline. The key management issue is that metropolitan modes still carried 92.06% of 2025 patronage, while Metropolitan Train and Metropolitan Tram remained below 80% of their 2019 baseline.

The priority is therefore not simply to identify the strongest or weakest recovery. The priority is to connect recovery gap with system share and then decide what operational evidence is needed next.

## What The Dashboard Shows

- Overall patronage trend from 2018 to 2026 year-to-date.
- 2025 patronage and recovery against 2019.
- Mode-specific recovery ranking.
- 2025 mode share.
- Action-priority matrix combining recovery gap and mode share.
- Recommendations for next evidence: corridor, station, route, reliability, traffic flow, signal delay and land-use context.
- Latest 12 months of mode-level patronage data.
- Data quality, limitations and next analytical steps.

## Interpretation

The recovery pattern is not uniform. Regional/VLine services show stronger recovery against 2019 than metropolitan rail and tram. This may reflect changes in travel behaviour, service patterns, regional demand, event travel, commuting patterns or other contextual factors.

The dashboard does not explain causation by itself. It gives a strategic evidence base for asking better follow-up questions. The strongest next question is a metropolitan corridor and route diagnostic, because large-share metropolitan modes are where the remaining recovery gap matters most to the total system.

## Recommended Next Analysis

1. Combine patronage with service frequency and reliability data.
2. Add corridor, route, station or stop-level datasets where available.
3. Compare patronage trends with population growth and land-use change.
4. Add road traffic volume, travel-time reliability and intersection delay data where bus or tram performance interacts with road conditions.
5. Review signal-priority opportunities for tram and bus corridors where demand and delay evidence both support action.
6. Review policy, fare, major project and event context for months with unusual patterns.
7. Prepare separate metropolitan and regional deep dives for stakeholder discussion.

## Suggested Stakeholder Questions

- Which modes are driving the gap between current patronage and 2019 baseline?
- Are weak-recovery modes concentrated in particular corridors, times or user segments?
- Do service levels, reliability or land-use change explain part of the pattern?
- For tram and bus corridors, do road congestion, signal delay or travel-time reliability explain part of the patronage pattern?
- For V/Line corridors, does above-baseline recovery create capacity, reliability or access risks?
- What additional evidence is needed before recommending a policy or investment response?

## How This Supports Policy Work

This project demonstrates a repeatable policy-analysis workflow: define the question, clean the data, calculate transparent metrics, visualise the result, communicate the limitations and recommend the next evidence step. It is structured so a manager can move from dashboard monitoring to a specific work program: metro diagnostic, regional capacity monitoring, bus/tram road-interface analysis and monthly executive reporting.
